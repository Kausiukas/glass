What you are looking at

A slab with two faces and glass in between

Each pixel follows a ray through the slab: into the front face, a letter wall or a button, across the body of the glass, out of the back face, then onto the beach behind. Nothing here is a drop shadow or a layered copy.

  • Front faceReflects the sky, the clouds and the sun, and at grazing angles the beach itself. The reflection grows toward the edges, as on real glass.
  • LettersCut by a gouge (rounded floor) or a V-chisel (sharp crease), at the wall angle you set. The rim is chipped and slightly uneven, and the walls keep faint tool marks. The finish runs from polished to matte grey, with an optional gold-leaf or white-enamel fill.
  • ButtonsRaised glass on the same slab. The main one is tinted glass, darkest where it is thickest. Both rise on hover and press down when clicked.
  • BodyThe beach shifts and bends with the thickness, and colours split slightly at bevels and cuts. Light that travels farther through the glass picks up its tint. Tiny air bubbles sit at different depths.
  • Back faceReflects a faint, offset copy of the letters back up through the glass.
  • ShadowGlass bends sunlight into bright lines (caustics). The edges, the cuts and the domed buttons throw them onto the beach, and the blue button throws blue light.
